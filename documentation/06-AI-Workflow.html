06 · The AI workflow

What the AI decides, what the analyst decides

Harbor acts on its own only where the action can be undone. Irreversible actions need a person to confirm them. Every action is logged in the audit trail.

Harbor AI's actions
Decides, each with an undo
  • The severity
  • The one-line summary
  • Auto-closing low-risk alerts, listed under Auto-closed with Reopen case
  • Reversible fixes, such as revoking a share link, with Restore link
Only suggests
  • The verdict, in Harbor's call
  • Recommended next steps
  • Grouping alerts into an incident, under Harbor suggests
  • Irreversible actions, which need a named confirmation
Prioritization logic

Alerts are listed by severity, with alerts on high-value assets first. Each alert has a Priority and Reasoning section.

WeighsSensitivity of the data
WeighsExposure: internal, external, public
WeighsDistance from the user's and device's baseline
WeighsWhether the asset is marked critical
Listed firstAlerts on high-value assets are in the first section shown before other alerts 

Splunk is the precedent: its urgency combines the finding's severity with the priority of the asset or identity, so a medium alert on a crown-jewel system can outrank a high one elsewhere.

Confirming, restoring and marking as expected

Harbor's call-to-action has three buttons.

ConfirmRestore linkMark as expected
  • Confirm: keep what Harbor did and set the verdict to True positive.
  • Restore link: bring the link back, then ask whether to edit the response or change the verdict.
  • Mark behavior or pattern as expected: sets the verdict to Expected activity and opens 'Teach Harbor'.
What is reversible
Reversible

Harbor does it at once and the alert shows what was done. The user can use an undo action or reopen a closed alert.

Not reversible

A flag icon and the a labeled button describing which action is being performed, along with a confirmation modal.
