        The REACT framework: five things an alert needs

        From a peer-reviewed study of SOC practitioners presented at USENIX Security 2022 by Bushra Alahmadi, Louise Axon and Ivan Martinovic at Oxford. The paper closes with five properties an alarm needs. Read against the products above, it doubles as a scoring sheet.

        PropertyWhat the paper asks forWho in this set does it
        ReliableFeatures and models that hold up in practice, with benign triggers measured separately from false positives, and analyst feedback fed back into the modelPurview’s Teach agent and Nightfall’s apply-to-all are the only two that feed a correction back. Nobody measures benign triggers separately.
        ExplainableThe system communicates its reasoning in terms an analyst understands, so trust can be calibratedPurview writes a reason per row. Cyera grades its own match. Varonis explains and refuses to conclude. The one-sentence description the paper calls unusable is exactly what Falcon ships.
        AnalyticalSupport the analyst’s reasoning rather than replace it with full automationVaronis is the clearest case: branches and commands, no verdict. Purview’s agent sits at the other end, deciding and inviting correction.
        ContextualCarry context about the network, the assets, the customer and outside sources, down to working hoursSplunk mixes asset priority into urgency. Varonis’s Alert Insights judge the account. Defender marks a Critical asset. Netskope carries none of this.
        TransferableAdapt to each environment instead of one size fitting allNobody in this set visibly adapts a model per tenant. Tuning is manual everywhere, through policy edits, suppression rules or taught properties.

        What this leaves Harbor to argue with

        Six things in this set, counting the special mentions, are genuinely good and underused. Each is a specific borrowing, not a theme, and each one has a visible cost that an assignment should be willing to name.

        Grade the detection, not just the content

        Cyera's MATCH SUMMARY states whether the rule was right to fire, separately from what the document is. Nobody else does it. Cost: a wrong self-grade is more damaging than a wrong summary, because it sounds like a verdict.

        Show the untriaged bucket

        Purview's Not triaged (324) tells an analyst where the AI stopped. Cost: it makes the product's limits the first thing a buyer sees.

        Split status from disposition

        Splunk keeps where the work is apart from what it turned out to be, and separates a wrong rule from wrong data. Cost: two fields to fill at close instead of one.

        Make correction produce a rule

        Purview's Teach agent and Nightfall's Apply to all identical findings turn one disagreement into lasting tuning. Cost: a careless correction silences a class of real alerts, so it needs an undo. Nightfall provides one.

        Name confidence, do not score it

        Nightfall uses Possible, Likely, Very Likely, each with a glyph. A percentage invites false precision that the model cannot support.

        Make rows self-describing

        Falcon repeats the field label inside every cell, so a row can be judged at page 300 without the header. Cost: visual density, and it only pays above a few hundred rows.

        The gap nobody fills. Varonis explains and refuses to decide. Purview decides and lets you correct the decision. Cyera decides about its own rule. None of them shows the analyst what the AI checked and failed to confirm, which is the thing an analyst has to know before they trust a verdict at all. That is an opening, and it is the one Harbor's brief points at.

        Scored on what was observed

        This is not a usability score. No usability test was run and no reviewer rating is comparable across sites, so inventing a number would be a guess dressed as a result. Instead, each product is checked against eight things this teardown actually looked for on the alerts page, and the score is simply how many it was seen to do. A dash means it was observed not to, and a question mark means the pages and screens read here did not settle it. Green is observed, red is observed absent, gray is not established. Only the six products covered in full are scored; the seven special mentions had too many unestablished cells to compare fairly.
