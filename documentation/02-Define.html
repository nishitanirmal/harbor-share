Users
User 1 The SOC analyst

Triages and remediates. Within one queue the range runs from a tier one analyst working from a runbook to a tier three analyst who writes queries daily.

User 2 The approver

A manager or overview role. Often a system owner approving an action on their system, rather than a security manager approving a verdict. The screens treat both.

Goals: Business, Stakeholder, User
Business, the Harbor team · 3
  • A comprehensive set of interventions that makes alerts a market leader
  • Know competitors' advantages and disadvantages, and outdo the benchmark
  • Provide stakeholders and users with a scalable, long-term, and consistent product solution
Stakeholder, CISO or team head · 6
  • Faster triage and remediation
  • Maximum accuracy in the information the team decides on
  • No blind spots in security left unattended
  • Defensible closure: have an audit trail of all actions
  • Full coverage: leave nothing unassigned
  • Trust in the AI at platform level
User, the analyst · 8
  • Less fatigue in triage and remediation
  • Faster time to resolution
  • Better decision-making capability and information
  • Provision to use advanced capabilities, if able
  • A workflow that improves with use
  • To be able to trust the AI while also remaining skeptical and doing due diligence
  • Not being blamed for an action that cannot be undone
  • Handing work over cleanly
Assumptions
  • In the absence of user research, I made certain assumptions in order to carry the design process forward
    • A few hundred open alerts a day1440 px base widthSame person triages and remediatesAnalysts can teach HarborVendor figures
    • Scale of alerts. Assumed from Matters.AI's own case studies: Credit Saison, thousands of SaaS users on a hybrid device fleet; Groww, over 100 petabytes across distributed cloud environments. I designed for a handful of analysts working a few hundred open alerts a day. Designing for scale shows rationale for 20 and 10,000 endpoints.
    • Screen-size. Based on typical office desktop screens 1440 wide is the base. 
    • Triage and remediation are done by the same person. While designing for the user persona, I realized that there could be three main roles in this workflow: triage, remediation, and overseeing/supervision. I have assumed that L1 and L2's actions are done by the same person in a hypothetical small team.
    • Analysts can teach Harbor. A reason given in Teach Harbor is assumed to change how Harbor treats similar alerts later. The prototype shows the dialog and the log entry, not the learning itself.
    • All figures are vendor-reported. Every performance number here comes from competitors' public material and is unverified.

    Not measured in this presentation.

    1. Time to first decision on an alert
    2. Proportion of alerts closed with a reason recorded
    3. Rate of AI recommendations rejected, and why
    4. Proportion of alerts never opened at all. The honest measure of fatigue
